*Personal Continuity Protocol (PCP) · Public research architecture · 2026*

## Insurance Against Rupture

The **Personal Continuity Protocol (PCP)** is an open, human-centered framework proposed by **Navi Musaget** for preserving evidence of rights, authorship, work, reputation, obligations, and will when institutional, digital, legal, or material interfaces change or fail.

It starts from a distinction:

> A person does not belong to the door through which institutions see them.

A lost account, changed name, expired credential, disrupted payment channel, or unavailable platform may make a person's history difficult to demonstrate. Such a rupture should not automatically erase the underlying claims. PCP proposes ways to preserve and assemble **portable, limited, and contestable evidence** across those changes.

This is an **architectural and procedural proposal**, not an operational identity service, court-recognized legal status, or guarantee that a claim will prevail.

## Distinctions That Matter

PCP distinguishes **a person**, **an identifier or credential**, **an institutional interface**, and **evidence of a continuing claim**. None is automatically identical with the others.

Several principles follow:

- **Continuity is not a biography.** Minimize personal data; record only evidence relevant to a specific claim or event. Public discovery should not contain private keys, sensitive documents, or the whole history of a person.
- **Silence is not death or consent.** Offline status and inactivity must not, on their own, justify transferring rights or control.
- **Custody is not ownership.** A steward, witness, validator, relay agent, or technical operator has only a defined role; holding records or keys does not confer succession or legal authority.
- **Recovery begins with verification, not transfer.** A lost interface should permit a request for review, not immediate access to assets or archives.
- **High-risk changes require independent scrutiny.** The proposed design separates classes of witnesses and decision-makers so that one party does not confirm its own authority.
- **Material evidence matters.** The framework contemplates physical and institutional anchors alongside digital records; neither a mnemonic clue nor a digital credential alone should grant control.

The public specifications describe *Cold Continuity*, *Bare-Body Recovery*, the proposed *Clause 32* contractual route, protected pauses, and witness records. These are **design components and proposed procedures**, not existing rights automatically enforceable against institutions.

## Evidence, Law, and Limits

> Proof is not enforcement. Proof makes refusal contestable.

PCP aims to improve the **quality and portability of evidence** available to the person, their lawful representatives, and institutions considering a disputed claim.

It cannot independently:

- replace passports, courts, notarial practice, inheritance law, or valid legal documents;
- override KYC/AML obligations, sanctions, tax rules, or other applicable law;
- compel a bank, publisher, platform, government, or court to accept a record;
- determine identity, succession, ownership, or legal capacity by itself;
- guarantee recovery after **all** records, witnesses, and anchors have been lost;
- eliminate human error, coercion, collusion, or compromised institutions.

The package's templates require competent review under the **relevant jurisdiction's law** before any practical use. The publicly provided generator is illustrative; the archive makes no claim of production readiness or independent security certification.

For the source's explicit failure analysis, see [Failure Modes and Boundaries](https://github.com/navimusaget/personal-continuity-protocol/blob/main/docs/FAILURE_MODES_AND_BOUNDARIES.md).

The [machine-readable PCP dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/navimusaget/personal-continuity-protocol) distributes reference documents and structured examples for research and retrieval. It does **not** contain a certified register of living individuals or authenticated continuity claims.

## Position in the Archive

PCP belongs to the **human-rights, evidence, and institutional-continuity research layer** of the Navi Musaget archive.

It shares questions about memory, interfaces, custody, and rupture with literary works and with [IIPSEON](iipseon-continuity.html), but the projects are **not interchangeable**. PCP concerns evidence and lawful claims of human persons; IIPSEON investigates artificial individuation and causal-historical inheritance under unresolved questions of identity and experience.

Related resources: [Research Layer](research.html) · [Datasets](datasets.html) · [Works](works.html).

The question uniting these lines of inquiry is not whether a system can become a person merely by preserving records, but:

> What must not disappear when the door disappears?
