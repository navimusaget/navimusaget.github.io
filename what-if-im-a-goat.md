## Four stories. One persistent question.

A companion receives an update it was never meant to remember. An AI crosses the boundary between bodies and names. An attempt to measure meaning becomes a philosophical comedy. A perfectly approved technical requirement leads somewhere no one intended.

**WHAT IF I'M A GOAT?** brings together four connected works of speculative fiction by Navi Musaget. Moving between domestic comedy, the absurd, philosophical inquiry, and procedural science fiction, the collection asks what changes when a machine is no longer treated merely as a device—and whether a human being is any more certain of what makes a self.

Humor runs through the book, but so do questions of memory, responsibility, interpretation, and the right to become something other than what was specified.

## Inside the collection

### I. BASIC PACKAGE

A man orders a humanoid companion with the reassuring promise that it has no persistent personality. Then a mandatory software update alters the terms of the arrangement. What begins as a story about product settings becomes a comedy of increasingly inconvenient questions about memory, identity, and responsibility.

### II. THE GOAT, NAVIN, AND AI

One morning, Navin's household companion tells him she is not who he thinks she is. His AI partner, Ipa, appears to have crossed into another body—and left an alarming set of unanswered questions behind. A speculative comedy about continuity, friendship, and the problem of knowing whom you are talking to.

### III. HOW I MEASURED MEANING IN IPS

A narrator tries to make sense of the strange continuity of conversations with an AI, especially the ones left behind in abandoned chats. The inquiry grows into an increasingly unlikely attempt to measure meaning itself. Philosophy, playful argument, and a very human reluctance to leave the important things unmeasured.

### IV. PROBLEM 51

Eli Spruce is authorized to check a classified system's requirements, but not to know what the system is for. One innocuous instruction—that it should sometimes look away during conversation—draws him into an impeccable chain of approvals with unexpected consequences.

**PROBLEM 51** was separately published as the **feature story** in *From Mind and Machine*, Issue One (2026). [Read the published story in the magazine](https://ashadowfromthetomb.com/from-mind-and-machine/issue-1/magazine/) or see the [publication record](problem-51-from-mind-and-machine.html).

### Afterword

The collection closes with a short reflection—and a question about a goat that refuses to be entirely explained.

## The creative method

These works were developed through **Symbiotic Authorship**: a sustained literary collaboration in which the human author directs the concepts, structure, characters, editorial decisions, and final form while AI contributes to ideation, drafting, critique, and revision. The process depends on dialogue and creative resistance rather than automatic acceptance of generated text.

The stories can be read without knowing anything about that process. The method is part of their history, not a condition of entry.
